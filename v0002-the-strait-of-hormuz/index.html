A note on simplifying. This is a 10-minute story about a strait with seven hundred years of history and a fast-moving 2026 crisis, so we rounded numbers, used comparisons for scale and left a lot out. Figures from 2026 come from early reports and may be revised. Where sources disagree, we used the more cautious figure and say so below. Found a mistake? Tell us in the video's comments and we will fix this page.

0:34An ordinary morning

0:34
A gap in the sea, about fifty kilometers wide.

The IEA gives the narrowest point of the strait as 29 nautical miles (54 km). Other sources measure the narrowest stretch of open water at 21 nautical miles (39 km), so we rounded to "about fifty".

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
0:43
an elbow of water between Iran and Oman

The strait lies between Iran on its north shore and Oman's Musandam peninsula, an exclave cut off from the rest of Oman, on its south shore.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
  • Wikipedia. Strait of Hormuz. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz

1:15A sea with one door

1:15
Rivers come in from the north, deserts wrap around the rest, and the only way out to the open ocean is through here.

The Persian Gulf is almost enclosed. Its only link to the open ocean is the Strait of Hormuz, which leads to the Gulf of Oman. The main river inflow is the Shatt al-Arab, where the Tigris and Euphrates arrive from the north.

  • Encyclopaedia Britannica. Persian Gulf.
1:25
on average about fifty meters deep, roughly the height of a fifteen-story building

The Gulf's average depth is about 50 m, with a maximum of about 90 m. Comparing 50 m to a fifteen-story building is our illustration, at roughly 3.3 m per floor.

  • Wikipedia. Persian Gulf. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf
  • New World Encyclopedia. Persian Gulf.
1:38
this was the edge of an ancient ocean full of tiny plankton. They died, sank, got buried, and slowly cooked into oil and gas

Most Gulf oil and gas formed from marine plankton buried in sediments along the margin of the ancient Tethys Ocean, on the Arabian Plate. We kept the wording general.

  • Encyclopaedia Britannica. Persian Gulf; Petroleum.
1:51
hold roughly half of the world's proven oil reserves

Using 2025 country shares of proven reserves: Saudi Arabia 15.1%, Iran 11.8%, Iraq 8.2%, UAE 6.4%, Kuwait 5.8% and Qatar 1.4% add up to about 48.8%, hence "roughly half".

  • Worldometer. Oil reserves by country (2025). worldometers.info/oil/oil-reserves-by-country
  • Our World in Data / Energy Institute. Oil reserves.
1:54
they share the largest natural gas field ever found

Iran and Qatar share one field, called South Pars in Iran and North Dome in Qatar. It holds about 51 trillion cubic metres of gas in place, the largest known gas accumulation.

  • IEA data, via Wikipedia. South Pars / North Dome gas-condensate field.
2:13
one in and one out, each less than four kilometers wide, with a buffer in between

Ships use two marked traffic lanes, one inbound and one outbound. Each is about 2 nautical miles (3.7 km) wide, separated by a buffer zone of about 2 nautical miles.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf

2:37The jewel in the ring

2:37
Seven hundred years ago, the star here was a tiny, barren island called Hormuz.

The island of Hormuz (Ormus) was a barren rock with almost no fresh water. Its trading kingdom was at its height from the 14th to the 16th century, so "seven hundred years ago" is a round figure.

  • Encyclopaedia Britannica. Hormuz.
  • Wikipedia. Kingdom of Ormus. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ormus
2:48
one of the richest ports of its age: Indian spices, Chinese porcelain, Persian silk and Arabian horses, all changing hands right here

Medieval Hormuz was an entrepot where goods from India, China, Persia and Arabia were traded onward by sea and land. The horse trade to India was a major business.

  • Encyclopaedia Britannica. Hormuz.
  • Wikipedia. Kingdom of Ormus. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ormus
2:59
Marco Polo passed through.

Marco Polo's route is dated to about 1272 and again about 1293 and went through the port of Hormuz on the mainland, an older site than the island city. We kept "passed through" deliberately general.

  • Encyclopaedia Britannica. Marco Polo.
3:02
if the world were a golden ring, Hormuz would be the jewel in it

A traditional saying, repeated in many histories of the port. We present it as "an old saying" without attributing it to a person.

  • ISCTE-IUL Centro de Estudos Internacionais (CEI), blog posts tagged Hormuz. blog.cei.iscte-iul.pt/tag/hormuz
3:08
Then, in 1507, Portuguese ships showed up. Their commander, Afonso de Albuquerque

A Portuguese fleet under Afonso de Albuquerque first arrived at Hormuz in 1507. It left, then came back and secured the island in 1515.

  • Wikipedia. Portuguese intervention in Hormuz (1514-1515).
  • Encyclopaedia Britannica. Afonso de Albuquerque.
3:16
you don't have to control an entire ocean, just its doors

This paraphrases Albuquerque's strategy of holding a few key ports and straits, such as Hormuz and Malacca (and attempting Aden), rather than the sea itself. It is our summary, not a quotation from him.

  • Encyclopaedia Britannica. Afonso de Albuquerque.
3:20
By 1515 Portugal held Hormuz, built a fortress and taxed the traffic for about a century, until 1622, when Persia, with help from English ships, took it back.

Portugal held Hormuz from 1515 and built a fortress on the island. A Persian force supported by English East India Company ships captured it in 1622. From 1515 to 1622 is 107 years, hence "about a century".

  • Wikipedia. Portuguese intervention in Hormuz (1514-1515).
  • Wikipedia. Capture of Ormuz (1622).
3:44
In the 1980s, during the Iran-Iraq War, hundreds of ships were attacked, and the United States ended up escorting tankers through the Gulf.

During the "Tanker War" of 1984 to 1988, both sides attacked commercial shipping in the Gulf. In 1987 and 1988 the US Navy escorted reflagged Kuwaiti tankers (Operation Earnest Will), about 259 ships in 127 convoys.

  • United States Institute of Peace. The Iran Primer, the Tanker War. iranprimer.usip.org/node/6146
  • GlobalSecurity.org. Operation Earnest Will.

4:06Not just oil

4:06
About twenty million barrels of oil and fuel pass through here every day. That's about a fifth of all the oil the world uses, and roughly a quarter of all the oil that travels by sea.

The EIA puts flows at about 20 million barrels per day in 2024, close to 20% of world petroleum liquids consumption. The IEA gives 20 million barrels per day in 2025, around 25% of seaborne oil trade.

  • U.S. EIA. World Oil Transit Chokepoints.
  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
4:18
more than a thousand Olympic swimming pools of oil

Our arithmetic: 20 million barrels x 159 litres = about 3.18 billion litres. An Olympic pool holds about 2.5 million litres, so that is roughly 1,270 pools.

  • Calculation from the EIA flow figure above.
4:24
Most of it heads east, to China, India, Japan and Korea.

About 80% of the oil and fuel that passes through the strait goes to Asian buyers.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
4:31
Qatar, a country about the size of Jamaica

Qatar covers about 11,581 km² and Jamaica about 10,991 km².

  • CIA World Factbook. Qatar; Jamaica.
4:37
It chills the gas to minus 162 degrees Celsius, until it shrinks about six hundred times into a liquid

Natural gas turns liquid at about -162 °C (-260 °F), and as liquefied natural gas (LNG) it takes up about 1/600th of the volume.

  • U.S. EIA. Liquefied natural gas explained.
4:47
About a fifth of all the liquefied gas traded on Earth goes through this strait.

The IEA estimates that 19 to 20% of global LNG trade passes through Hormuz, almost all of it from Qatar.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
4:52
It heats homes and runs power plants from Tokyo to Lahore.

About 90% of the LNG leaving the Gulf goes to Asia. Pakistan, Bangladesh and India are among the most dependent buyers, with roughly two-thirds of their LNG coming through the strait.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
4:56
And there is no pipeline around it.

The IEA notes there is no alternative export route for the LNG. The bypass pipelines that exist (see "When the door closes") carry crude oil only.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
5:04
most of the world's nitrogen fertilizer is made from natural gas

Nitrogen fertilizers start from ammonia, and about 72% of the world's ammonia was made from natural gas in 2020 (most of the rest from coal).

  • IEA. Ammonia Technology Roadmap (2021).
5:15
About a third of all the urea traded in the world passes through Hormuz.

Estimates range from 30 to 35%: IFPRI says about 35% of global urea flows, Fertilizers Europe 34% (2024), and the FAO 30 to 35%.

  • IFPRI. A narrow strait, global consequences: the Hormuz Strait and fertilizer markets (2026). ifpri.org/event/a-narrow-strait-global-consequences-hormuz-strait-and-fertilizer-markets
  • Fertilizers Europe (2024); FAO (2026), urea trade estimates.
5:20
synthetic nitrogen helps feed roughly half of humanity

Erisman and colleagues estimated that nitrogen fertilizer made by the Haber-Bosch process supported about 48% of the world's population in 2008.

  • Erisman, J. W. et al. (2008). How a century of ammonia synthesis changed the world. Nature Geoscience 1, 636-639; shown by Our World in Data.
5:34
a giant magnet has to be kept at about minus 269 degrees, cooled by liquid helium

Liquid helium boils at 4.2 kelvin, which is -269 °C. It keeps an MRI scanner's superconducting magnet cold enough to work.

  • Standard physical data: boiling point of helium-4 (4.2 K).
5:44
roughly a third of global supply, and most of it leaves by sea

The Congressional Research Service put Qatar at about 30% of world helium capacity, and 2026 reports say "about a third". That most of it leaves by sea is our inference: after the land border closed in 2017, shipping was its only route out.

  • Congressional Research Service. Report R45281 (helium). congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R45281/R45281.6.pdf
5:48
In 2017, when Qatar's neighbors closed its only land border, both of its helium plants had to shut down, and buyers around the world scrambled for other suppliers.

In June 2017 Saudi Arabia and its allies cut ties with Qatar and closed its only land border. Both of Qatar's helium plants stopped, and buyers such as Linde turned to the US, Algeria and Australia.

  • Reuters via Manufacturing.net (2017). A blockade of Qatar could threaten the world's helium supply. manufacturing.net/news/2017/06/blockade-qatar-could-threaten-worlds-helium-supply
  • GEP (2017), analysis of the Qatar helium disruption.
6:02
When the Gulf cleans sulfur out of its oil and gas, it ends up with mountains of yellow powder.

Most of the world's sulfur is recovered as a by-product when sulfur is removed from crude oil and natural gas. The Gulf's large sour oil and gas output is why it is such a big producer.

  • USGS. Mineral Commodity Summaries: Sulfur.
6:08
Nearly half of all the sulfur shipped by sea comes from this region.

S&P Global estimated about 50% of seaborne sulfur in March 2026, IFPRI about 45% passing through Hormuz, and Kpler gave a similar figure. We said "nearly half".

  • S&P Global Commodity Insights (March 2026), sulfur supply and the Hormuz disruption.
  • IFPRI (2026), Hormuz and fertilizer markets.
  • Kpler (2026), sulfur trade flows.
6:12
That sulfur becomes sulfuric acid, which turns rock into more fertilizer and helps pull metals like copper out of ore.

Sulfur is turned into sulfuric acid, whose largest use is making phosphate fertilizer from phosphate rock. It is also used to leach copper from ore (solvent extraction and electrowinning, SX-EW).

  • USGS. Mineral Commodity Summaries: Sulfur.

6:46When the door closes

6:46
Then in 2026, during the war between Iran, Israel and the United States, it stopped being one.

Hostilities began on 28 February 2026. We name the countries without describing the course of the war, which is outside the scope of this video.

  • Al-Monitor (April 2026), reporting on shipping through the strait during the war.
6:53
Traffic through the strait, normally well over a hundred ships a day, collapsed to a handful.

About 140 vessels a day passed through before the war began; afterwards ship-tracking data showed only a handful of daily passages.

  • Al-Monitor (April 2026), about 140 daily passages before 28 February.
  • AFP / MarineTraffic ship-tracking data (2026).
6:59
The International Energy Agency called it the largest supply disruption in the history of the oil market.

The IEA's March 2026 Oil Market Report described the disruption as the largest supply disruption in the history of the oil market.

  • IEA Oil Market Report, March 2026, reported by BNN Bloomberg (12 March 2026). bnnbloomberg.ca/business/2026/03/12/world-faces-largest-ever-oil-supply-disruption-on-middle-east-war-iea-says
7:05
In a single month, the price of oil jumped by about half.

Brent crude rose about 51% during March 2026.

  • CNBC (21 April 2026). Oil prices and the Iran war. cnbc.com/2026/04/21/oil-price-iran-war-middle-east.html
7:15
Pakistan, which runs much of its power on Qatari gas, had to cut electricity for hours at a time.

After QatarEnergy declared force majeure on LNG deliveries, Pakistan introduced load-shedding of up to five hours a day in April 2026.

  • Arab News (April 2026), Pakistan load-shedding and LNG shortages.
  • S&P Global Commodity Insights (2026), QatarEnergy force majeure.
7:22
Fertilizer prices jumped

Fertilizer prices rose sharply in 2026 because so much urea, ammonia and sulfur normally moves through the strait. The delay before this reaches food prices is the usual pattern, not a figure we measured.

  • IFPRI. Iran war's impacts on global fertilizer markets (2026).
7:26
Helium got scarce again.

Helium prices rose in 2026 as Qatar's output was disrupted, the second helium squeeze after the one in 2017.

  • Radiology Business (2026), reporting on helium prices and supply.
7:40
Saudi Arabia built a pipeline across the desert to the Red Sea, and the UAE built one to a port outside the strait.

Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline carries crude to Yanbu on the Red Sea. The UAE's Habshan-Fujairah pipeline carries crude to Fujairah on the Gulf of Oman, outside the strait.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
7:48
Together they could carry roughly another three and a half to five and a half million barrels a day. Useful. But that's a detour for about a quarter of the oil, at best.

The IEA estimates 3.5 to 5.5 million barrels per day of unused bypass capacity. Against about 20 million barrels per day through the strait, the top of that range is 5.5/20, or roughly a quarter.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
8:00
Drones have hit their pumping stations, and the ports at their ends

In May 2019 drones hit two pumping stations on the East-West pipeline and the line was shut. In 2026 one of its 11 pumping stations was damaged in April, the Fujairah oil port was struck by drones in March, and the Yanbu SAMREF refinery was hit on 19 March. We name no attacker. The Habshan-Fujairah pipeline itself was not hit, only its port.

  • AP via Fox News (14 May 2019). Drones hit Saudi pumping stations. foxnews.com/world/saudi-arabia-pipeline-drone-yemen-houthi
  • Reuters via New Straits Times (April 2026). Saudi says East-West pipeline facilities restored. nst.com.my/amp/business/economy/2026/04/1416066/saudi-says-east-west-pipeline-energy-facilities-restored-after
  • Reuters via Baird Maritime (16 March 2026). ADNOC oil loading halted at Fujairah. bairdmaritime.com/amp/story/shipping/ports/adnoc-oil-loading-still-halted-at-fujairah-as-other-loadings-resume
  • MEED. Fujairah oil hub targeted in fresh drone strike. meed.com/fujairah-oil-hub-targeted-in-fresh-drone-strike
  • Marine Insight (March 2026). SAMREF refinery in Yanbu hit. marineinsight.com/iran-targets-saudi-aramco-exxon-samref-refinery-in-yanbu-briefly-disrupting-red-sea-oil-flows
8:04
one attack shut the Saudi line for more than a week

A drone attack on 10 and 11 September 2026 damaged 3 of the pipeline's 11 pumping stations, and flows restarted after about 11 days. Saudi Arabia said the drones were launched from Iraq.

  • Reuters via Baird Maritime (22 September 2026). Red Sea crude flows poised to rebound as Saudi pipeline restarts. bairdmaritime.com/offshore/transport/red-sea-crude-flows-poised-to-rebound-as-saudi-pipeline-restarts
  • Al Jazeera (11 September 2026). Saudi Arabia says East-West pipeline hit by drones launched from Iraq. aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/11/saudi-arabia-says-east-west-pipeline-hit-by-drones-launched-from-iraq
8:07
Out on the Red Sea, rebels in Yemen have fired on tankers.

Houthi forces claimed attacks on Saudi tankers in the Red Sea in July 2026. Since 2023 more than 100 merchant ships have been targeted in the Red Sea and nearby waters.

  • Reuters (23 July 2026). Houthis claim attacks on Saudi tankers in the Red Sea. hawaiitribune-herald.com/2026/07/23/nation-world-news/houthis-claim-attacks-on-saudi-tankers-in-red-sea-raising-risk-of-new-chokepoint-in-iran-war
  • Reuters factbox. Houthi attacks on merchant ships in the Red Sea (2023-2025). today.lorientlejour.com/article/1468697/factbox-houthi-attacks-on-merchant-ships-in-the-red-sea

8:24Why it never stays shut

8:24
Iran ships its own oil through it. So do Iraq, Kuwait and Qatar, who have almost no other way out.

Iran's main export terminals are inside the Gulf; its alternative at Jask, outside the strait, is not operational. Iraq, Kuwait and Qatar export almost everything through the strait.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026), export table. iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
8:31
China, the biggest buyer, needs it open.

China and India together take about 44% of the crude oil that passes through the strait, and China is the larger of the two.

  • IEA. Strait of Hormuz Factsheet (2026). iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/c8248eba-8689-46d9-ae4b-b858b59c0f1c/StraitofHormuz2026-Factsheet.pdf
8:34
under the law of the sea, ships have a right to pass through straits like this one

The UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (Part III) gives ships the right of "transit passage" through straits used for international navigation. Iran signed the convention but has not ratified it, so we say only "the law of the sea" and go no further.

  • United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (1982), Part III: Straits used for international navigation.
8:52
many countries keep emergency oil stocks, often enough for about three months of imports

IEA member countries must hold emergency oil stocks equal to at least 90 days of net imports. The agency was set up in 1974 after the 1973 oil crisis.

  • IEA. Oil security and emergency response: emergency stocks.
